## 2. THE PROBLEM

Recommended eyebrow:

*THE GAP BETWEEN A PROBLEM AND A SOLUTION*

### Great problems deserve more than complaints.

Across communities, people experience problems every day — unsafe water, difficult public services, infrastructure gaps, livelihood challenges, accessibility barriers and more. The problem is often not a lack of ideas. It is the missing bridge between the person who sees the problem and the people who can solve it.

### Today, the journey breaks in the middle.

- A real problem is reported.

- The report gets buried among many other requests.

- The right university or technical expertise may never see it.

- A promising idea may be discussed but never become a project.

- A prototype may be built without a path to a real pilot.

- A pilot may happen without consistent evidence of impact.

- A successful solution may never reach the next community facing the same problem.

### The missing connection

- Problem → the community knows what hurts.

- Capability → the right university or institution knows how to investigate.

- Execution → students, faculty and partners turn the challenge into a project.

- Enablement → industry / CSR can provide funding, testing, expertise or deployment support.

- Verification → evidence shows whether the intervention actually worked.

- Reuse → successful solutions become knowledge for the next similar challenge.

> **One-line section copy**

> Problem reported → ❌ gets lost<br>Problem → SETU → University → Industry → Pilot → Verified Impact

## 3. THE SETU LOOP

*Eyebrow: A CLOSED LOOP FOR REAL-WORLD CHANGE*

### From a lived problem to a verified outcome.

SETU connects the people who experience problems with the people, knowledge, infrastructure and resources needed to solve them.

### The loop

01  LISTEN — A citizen or community submits a real challenge with context and evidence.

02  STRUCTURE — SETU turns an unstructured report into a clear, actionable challenge.

03  VALIDATE — Moderators review evidence, feasibility, duplicates and priority.

04  MATCH — The challenge is matched to relevant university capabilities.

05  BUILD — Faculty and students form a team and develop a solution.

06  ENABLE — Industry / CSR can provide funding, expertise, testing or deployment support.

07  PILOT — The solution moves from prototype to a real-world field test.

08  VERIFY — Academic evidence, community confirmation and institutional validation establish the outcome.

09  REUSE — Successful solutions enter a controlled repository and can inform future challenges.

## 5. AI THAT ASSISTS. HUMANS THAT DECIDE.

*Eyebrow: HUMAN-GOVERNED INTELLIGENCE*

### AI should reduce the noise — not own the decision.

SETU uses AI where language, similarity and scale make manual workflows slow. The system recommends; authorized people retain the final say.

- Problem structuring — extract domain, urgency, affected population and useful context.

- Duplicate discovery — surface similar challenges using semantic, location and domain signals.

- Priority assistance — help moderators identify challenges that may need faster attention.

- Capability matching — rank likely university fits using a weighted, explainable model.

- Solution retrieval — surface relevant previously verified solutions.

- Solution Copilot — help university teams draft kickoff briefs, research directions and project plans.

> **Decision principle**

> Recommendation → Confidence → Evidence → Reasoning factors → Accept / Override → Audit

Use the phrase “AI-assisted” more often than “AI-powered” on the public website. It communicates that SETU remains accountable to people.

## 8. TRUST & VERIFICATION

*Eyebrow: PROVE IT. DON'T JUST CLAIM IT.*

### Impact is only meaningful when it can be checked.

SETU's verification model is designed around three perspectives. The exact verification policy can vary by program and domain.

### The Triangle of Trust

- Academic evidence — baseline measurements, post-pilot results, test reports and deployment evidence.

- Community confirmation — the affected community confirms that the intervention happened and reflects their experience.

- Institutional verification — an authorized institutional or program representative verifies deployment and supporting evidence.

### Verified Societal Outcomes

A Verified Societal Outcome (VSO) represents a challenge that has moved through solution execution and produced a real-world outcome that satisfies the program's verification conditions.

- Success is not the number of submissions.

- Success is not the number of AI recommendations.

- Success is not the number of projects created.

- Success is the number and quality of verified outcomes created.

> **Public-facing copy**

> We don't measure how many problems were submitted. We measure how many were moved toward meaningful, verified change.

## 15. FAQ / CREDIBILITY SECTION

### Is SETU just a grievance portal?

No. A grievance portal can stop at registration or administrative response. SETU is designed to connect community challenges with academic ownership, industry enablement, pilots and outcome verification.

### Why use AI?

AI is useful for structuring language, finding similar challenges, assisting prioritization, ranking capability matches and retrieving prior solutions. It does not receive irreversible governance authority.

### What if the AI is wrong?

Authorized moderators and coordinators can override recommendations. The recommendation, evidence and override can be logged for auditability.

### How do you prove impact?

Through a verification model combining academic evidence, community confirmation and institutional verification. Exact requirements can vary by program and domain.

### How do you stop spam?

Use appropriate identity verification, rate limits, CAPTCHA or bot controls, evidence requirements, duplicate detection and moderator review.

### How will citizen data be protected?

Use data minimization, role-based access, encryption, controlled media access, access logging and clear retention rules. Public views should avoid exposing unnecessary personal or precise location data.

### How does a university get matched?

SETU can rank institutions using capability signals such as domain expertise, faculty expertise, labs, equipment, previous work, capacity and geographic relevance.

### How does SETU scale?

The proposed architecture uses logical tenant boundaries, modular services, stateless application components, object storage and asynchronous processing so the platform can expand incrementally.

## 16. CONTENT & CLAIMS RULES

This is important for the public website and SIH demo. Keep the story ambitious, but never turn prototype assumptions into false partnership or impact claims.

- Label demo numbers as “Demo data” unless they come from verified deployment data.

- Do not imply formal partnerships with universities, government bodies or CSR organizations unless confirmed.

- Do not publish exact beneficiary counts, pilot results, similarity percentages or laboratory results without evidence.

- Use “proposed”, “illustrative”, “demo”, or “designed to” where a capability is not yet deployed.

- Keep the VSO number dynamic in the real product; avoid invented live statistics on the marketing site.

- Use real case studies only after the underlying outcome and permission to publish are verified.

## 17. COPY STYLE GUIDE

- Use short, confident sentences.

- Prefer verbs: listen, validate, match, build, enable, pilot, verify, reuse.

- Avoid corporate filler such as “revolutionize”, “disrupt”, “seamlessly”, or “next-generation” unless a concrete claim follows.

- Lead with human problems, then explain technology.

- Keep AI secondary to the outcome.

- Use bullets for complex workflows.

- Use one strong idea per section.

- Repeat the central loop visually so visitors remember it.

- Use “verified impact” only where the verification process is actually satisfied.

- Keep technical architecture mostly inside the product / documentation area, not the hero narrative.
